The key insight

Difficulty belongs to the card, not to you. So Claude can predict it before you’ve seen it.

FSRS tracks how hard each card is. Engram starts that number at Claude’s prediction instead of a blank guess, so the first reviews are already well spaced.

Every review then checks the prediction. Past accuracy goes back into the next extraction prompt, and Claude gets better at guessing.

This is the idea the whole design hangs on. Older algorithms like SM-2 tracked “ease” per learner, which drifted and got stuck. FSRS separates difficulty, a property of the material, from stability, a property of your memory. Difficulty is something a model can estimate from the text alone. So extraction hands the scheduler a head start, and the scheduler hands extraction a report card. Very hard cards, above 8 out of 10, get split automatically into smaller sub-concepts.
