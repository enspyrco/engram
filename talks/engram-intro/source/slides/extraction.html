Step 2 · Extraction

One page, three kinds of thing

Concepts

The ideas worth knowing, named and defined. These are the nodes.

Typed relationships

Not just “related”: this is a prerequisite of that, this is an analogy for that. These are the edges.

Quiz items

Questions and answers for each concept, plus Claude’s guess at how hard each one is to remember.

Seven relationship types, each drawn differently on the graph:

prerequisite

generalization

composition

enables

analogy

contrast

related to

Prerequisites get arrowheads and decide what unlocks next. Analogies are dashed, because they jump between topics.

Claude reads each wiki page and returns structured output: concepts, typed edges between them, and quiz items. The edge types matter. A prerequisite edge means you shouldn’t be quizzed on B until you’ve mastered A, so the graph decides what unlocks. An analogy edge connects ideas from different areas, which is where the most valuable and hardest learning lives.
