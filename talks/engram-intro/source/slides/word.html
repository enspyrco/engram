Liu, Ramirez et al., “Optogenetic stimulation of a hippocampal engram activates fear memory recall,” Nature 484 (2012)

Before the app, the name. An engram is the physical trace a memory leaves in the brain. Semon coined the word in 1904. Lashley spent decades cutting into rat brains looking for where a maze memory was stored and never found a single spot, because memories are distributed. In 2012 Tonegawa’s lab at MIT tagged the neurons that fired while a mouse learned to fear a box, then switched just those cells on with light in a different, safe box, and the mouse froze. A year later they used the same trick to plant a false memory. Two things carry over to Engram: a memory is a network, which is why the app is a graph, and every recall rewrites the memory, which is why spaced review works. And yes, there’s a backronym, Engram Notices Gaps, Recursively Awakens Meaning, but the real word came first.
