The risk

The marker must show its working

Any marker can be wrong in the kind direction: “yes, you said that,” when you didn’t. So every tick has to point at the words that earned it, the way a teacher underlines a sentence.

“Light is faster than sound, so it arrives first.”

The trap we hit

If the marker types out its own quote, we have to check that quote against your answer. Every “close enough” check we tried, ignoring capitals or punctuation, let through words you never wrote.

A marker that’s too generous breaks spaced repetition silently: you only find out when you’ve forgotten. So credit has to come with proof. The obvious way is to have Claude quote you. But then you need to check the quote is really in your answer, and loose matching kept letting made-up words through. Three rounds of adversarial review found a new hole each time. So we stopped matching loosely at all.
