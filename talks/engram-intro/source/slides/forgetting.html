The problem

You read it. A week later, most of it is gone.

Spaced repetition beats forgetting: review each idea just before you’d lose it, and every review makes it last longer.

But it has two costs nobody enjoys: someone has to write the cards, and the cards are islands. Nothing shows how the ideas connect.

Everyone knows this feeling. The fix has been known for a century: spaced repetition. Each review just before you forget makes the memory more durable, so the gaps between reviews can grow. The curve on the right is the idea, not measured data. The catch is the work: writing good cards is tedious, and flashcards are isolated facts. Engram goes after both costs.
